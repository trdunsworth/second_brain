I saw an article this morning, in the UDK about [11 more absurd laws in Kansas.](https://www.kansan.com/arts_and_culture/still-curious-about-kansas-craziest-laws-here-are-11-more/article_94174e6e-8164-4600-8d51-e03baecf6f9c.html) Number 4 got my attention. It was discussing the now-repealed funeral protest law in Overland Park. I could see the folly of privilege in the writer's comment: *though we’re not sure exactly how many Overland Park-based funerals people would want to protest in the first place.* It's great in the modern day that we don't worry about Westboro Baptist Church showing up to demonstrate at a funeral with their signs and bullhorns. I knew people who endured that and some who counter-demonstrated. This was a real ethical and philosophical dilemma for many of us. What were the acceptable limits of the First Amendment when they clashed with a family's right to mourn the loss of a loved one? 

Interesting that I heard back from the editor and it was "Thank you for your comments."
