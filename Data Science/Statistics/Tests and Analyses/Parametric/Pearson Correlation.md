# Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r)

Pearson's $r$ measures the strength and direction of the **linear** relationship between two continuous variables.

## 1. The Value of $r$
- **+1.0**: Perfect positive linear correlation.
- **0.0**: No linear correlation.
- **-1.0**: Perfect negative linear correlation.

## 2. Key Assumptions
- **Linearity**: The relationship must be a straight line.
- **Interval/Ratio Scale**: Variables must be continuous.
- **Normality**: Both variables should be normally distributed.
- **Homoscedasticity**: The "spread" of the residuals is constant.

## 3. Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$)
Squaring the correlation coefficient ($r^2$) gives the proportion of variance in one variable that is predictable from the other.
- *Example*: If $r = 0.7$, then $R^2 = 0.49$. This means 49% of the variance is shared.
