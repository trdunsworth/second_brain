# Inferential Statistics

Inferential statistics allows us to make generalizations about a large population based on a smaller, representative sample of that population.

## 1. Population vs. Sample
- **Population**: The entire group you want to draw conclusions about.
    - *Parameter*: A numerical characteristic of a population (e.g., population mean $\mu$).
- **Sample**: A specific group selected from the population.
    - *Statistic*: A numerical characteristic of a sample (e.g., sample mean $\bar{x}$).

## 2. Sampling Methods and Bias
To make accurate inferences, the sample must be representative.
- **Simple Random Sampling**: Every member has an equal chance of being selected.
- **Stratified Sampling**: Population is divided into subgroups (strata), and samples are taken from each.
- **Cluster Sampling**: Population is divided into clusters; entire clusters are randomly selected.
- **Sampling Bias**: Occurs when some members of the population are systematically more likely to be selected than others, leading to a non-representative sample.

## 3. Standard Error (SE)
While Standard Deviation measures the spread of individual data points, **Standard Error** measures the spread of the *sample statistic* across multiple hypothetical samples.

- **Standard Error of the Mean (SEM)**:
  $$\text{SE} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$
- **Insight**: As sample size ($n$) increases, the Standard Error decreases, meaning our sample mean becomes a more reliable estimate of the population mean.
