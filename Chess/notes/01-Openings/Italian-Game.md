# Italian Game - Complete Guide

> **Aggression**: 35/100 (Classical)  
> **ECO**: C50-C59  
> **Study Priority**: #2 (White)  
> **Estimated Hours**: 15  
> **Best Against**: 1...e5 players, those avoiding theory  
> **Teaches**: Open game fundamentals, piece play, maneuvering

---

## Why Italian for You?

1. **Principled chess** - Control center, develop, castle
2. **Rich middlegames** - Not forced draws, full of play
3. **Teaches fundamentals** - Every world champion knew it
4. **Flexible aggression** - Giuoco Piano (solid) → Evans (wild)
5. **Avoids Ruy Lopez theory** - Less memorization

---

## Core Idea

```chess
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4
```

**Bc4** - Attacks f7, controls d5, develops actively.

Compare:
- **Ruy Lopez** (3.Bb5): Pressure on c6, more theory
- **Italian** (3.Bc4): Direct attack, open play, less theory

## Main Variations

### 1. Giuoco Piano (Main Line) - **Study This First**
```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.c3 Nf6 5.d3 d6 6.0-0 0-0 7.Nbd2 a5 8.a4
```
**Slow, maneuvering, classical.**
- c3-d4 pawn center planned
- Knights dance: Nd2-f1-g3/e3 or Nd2-b3-c5
- Pressure f7 with Bc4, Qe2, Re1

### 2. Giuoco Pianissimo (Ultra-Solid, Agg 20)
```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.d3
```
**Avoids ALL theory.** Just develop.
- d3, Nbd2, 0-0, Re1, Nf1-g3/e3
- Pure maneuvering
- Good vs booked opponents

### 3. Evans Gambit (Aggressive, Agg 70)
```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.b4!
```
**Sacrifice pawn for development + attack.**
- 4...Bxb4 5.c3 Ba5 6.d4
- Rapid development: Nf3, Bc4, 0-0, Qb3
- Attack f7: Bc4, Qb3, Ng5
- **Only if you want to win at all costs**

### 4. vs Two Knights Defense (3...Nf6)
```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Na5 6.Bb5+ c6 7.dxc6 bxc6 8.Be2
```
**Main line, sharp but principled.**
- White has two bishops, Black has pawn structure
- Maneuver: Nd2, c3, d4, 0-0
- Pressure on c6/d5

### 5. vs Hungarian (3...Be7)
```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Be7 4.c3 Nf6 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 d5 7.exd5 Nxd5 8.Nxd5 Qxd5 9.Nc3
```
**Solid for Black.** White has space, Black solid.

## Strategic Themes

### 1. The c3-d4 Pawn Center
**Your strategic goal.**
- c3 supports d4
- d4 controls e5/c5
- Knights love d4/e5 outposts
- **Timing**: When pieces developed, Black can't challenge

### 2. Knight Maneuvers (The Italian Dance)
```
Nd2 → f1 → g3/e3 (kingside)
Nd2 → b3 → c5/d4 (queenside)
Nf3 → h4 → f3 → e5 (rare)
```
- **Nf1-g3**: Eyes f5/h5, supports d4
- **Nf1-e3**: Controls d5/f5, supports f4
- **Nb3-c5**: Outpost, pressure

### 3. f7 Weakness
**Black's Achilles heel.**
- Bc4 eyes it
- Qe2/Qb3 + Bc4 = battery
- Ng5 threats
- ...Be7 defends but passive

### 4. Bishop Pair
**In Two Knights: You often get two bishops.**
- Open position favors bishops
- Avoid trading unless necessary
- Control both color complexes

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why Bad | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Premature d4 | Loses center control | Prepare: c3, Nbd2, Re1 first |
| Ng5 too early | Traxler counterattack (4...Bc5!) | Only in Two Knights main line |
| Ignoring ...d5 | Opens game for Black | Meet with exd5 or d4 |
| Trading bishops early | Lose bishop pair | Keep two bishops in open games |
| Auto-pilot Italian | Miss tactical shots | Check checks/captures/threats |
