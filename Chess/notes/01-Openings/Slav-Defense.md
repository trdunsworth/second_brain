# Slav Defense - Complete Guide

> **Aggression**: 15/100 (Solid)  
> **ECO**: D10-D19  
> **Study Priority**: #1 (Black vs 1.d4)  
> **Estimated Hours**: 12  
> **Best Against**: 1.d4/Queen's Gambit, Catalan, higher rated  
> **World Champions**: Kramnik, Anand, Carlsen, Euwe

---

## Why Slav for You?

1. **Solid & Flexible** - Supports d5, keeps c8-bishop free
2. **Avoids QGA problems** - No ...dxc4 issues
3. **World Champion repertoire** - Kramnik, Anand, Carlsen
4. **Transpositions** - Can reach Semi-Slav, QGD, etc.
5. **Clear plans** - ...dxc4, ...Bf5, ...e6, ...b5

---

## Core Idea

```chess
1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6
```

**c6 supports d5** and **doesn't block the c8-bishop** (unlike ...e6 in QGD).

Compare:
- **QGD** (1...e6): Bishop blocked, ...dxc4 problematic
- **Slav** (1...c6): Bishop free, ...dxc4 natural!

---

## Main Variations

### 1. Main Line (Most Common)
```
1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 dxc4 5.a4 Bf5
```

**Why Bf5?** Develops bishop **before** ...e6 blocks it.

**Continuation:**
```
6.Ne5 Nbd7 7.Nxc4 Qc7 8.g3 e5 9.dxe5 Nxe5 10.Bf4 Nxc4 11.Bxc4
```
**Result**: Solid equality. Black has:
- No weak pawns
- Good dark bishop (on f5 → e4/g6)
- Active pieces
- ...e5 break achieved

### 2. Semi-Slav (More Active, Agg 35)
```
1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 e6
```
**Hybrid Slav/QGD**. More complex, more winning chances.
**Main**: 5.e3 Nbd7 6.Bd3 dxc4 7.Bxc4 b5
**Study after Main Line mastered.**

### 3. Exchange Variation (3.cxd5)
```
1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6 3.cxd5 cxd5 4.Nc3 Nf6 5.Bf4 Nc6 6.e3 Bf5
```
**Symmetrical, drawish**. Good for "must not lose".
**Plan**: Develop, trade, equal endgame.

### 4. Slow Slav (4.e3)
```
1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.e3 Bf5 5.Nc3 e6 6.Nh4
```
White tries to trade your good bishop. **Don't allow Nh4 easily!**
**Response**: ...Be6 or ...Bg6 or ...g6.

---

## Strategic Themes

### 1. The ...dxc4 / ...Bf5 Core
**This is your main plan.**
- Take on c4 when White plays Nc3
- Develop Bf5 **before** ...e6
- White's a4 prevents ...b5, but you don't need it yet

### 2. The ...e5 Break
**Your main freeing move.**
- In Main Line: Achieved by move 8-9!
- Opens position for your pieces
- White's d4 becomes weak

### 3. Queenside Pressure (...b5)
**In Semi-Slav and some Main Lines:**
- ...b5 challenges White's a4
- Creates activity on c-file
- Can lead to ...b4 outpost

### 4. Bishop Pair / Good Bishop
- Your dark bishop (f5/g6/e4) is typically **good**
- White's dark bishop often blocked by his own pawns
- Trade YOUR bad pieces, keep good bishop

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why Bad | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| ...e6 before ...Bf5 | Blocks bishop! | **Bf5 first, then e6 if needed** |
| Passive ...Be6 | Bishop buried | Bf5 → g6/h5 active |
| Ignoring Nh4 threat | Loses good bishop | Meet Nh4 with ...Be6/Bg6/g6 |
| Premature ...b5 | Weakens c5/b5 | Only in Semi-Slav or prepared |
| Missing ...e5 timing | White consolidates | Break when pieces developed |

1. **Main Line Development**: `r1bq1rk1/pp2bppp/2np1n2/3p4/2PP4/2N1PN2/PP3PPP/R1BQKB1R w KQ - 0 1` → Black: ...dxc4, ...Bf5
2. **Semi-Slav**: `rnbq1rk1/pp2ppbp/3p1np1/2pP4/2P1P3/2N2N2/PP3PPP/R1BQKB1R w KQ - 0 1` → Black: ...e6, ...dxc4, ...b5
3. **Nh4 Defense**: `r1bq1rk1/pp2bppp/2np1n2/3p4/2PP1N2/2N2P2/PP3P1P/R1BQKB1R w KQ - 0 1` → Black meets Nh4
