# D&D 3.5e → 5.5e (2024) Conversion Guide

A working guide for porting a Dungeons & Dragons 3.5 edition character, adventure, or campaign into the 5.5e/2024 ruleset as published in the **System Reference Document 5.2.1** (CC BY 4.0).

> [!warning] Read this first
> This guide is a **rules translation aid**, not a character builder's replacement. 3.5e and 5.5e are structurally different games: 3.5e is built from granular modifiers, feat chains, and situational bonuses; 5.5e is built from **Advantage/Disadvantage, bounded accuracy, and a short list of named actions**. Most conversion errors come from trying to preserve 3.5e's *stacking modifiers* rather than its *character concept*. Convert the concept, not the numbers.

### Conventions used in this note

- **`> [!src]` callouts** carry the SRD citation for the rule being described.
- **SRD page numbers** refer to the printed page footers of [[SRD_CC_v5.2.1]] (each page ends with `N  System Reference Document 5.2.1`).
- Rules the SRD 5.2.1 **does not contain** are marked **[GAP]** and collected in [[#Known Gaps and Ambiguities]]. Do not assume they exist — check the 2024 *Player's Handbook* / *Dungeon Master's Guide* (not in this vault) or design a house rule.
- SRD 5.2.1 **capitalizes defined rules terms** (Advantage, Grappled, Attack action, Heavy armor). This note follows that convention so you can grep the SRD for the term later.
- 3.5e-side facts (XP tables, save formulas, etc.) are drawn from the standard 3.5e rules and are marked **[3.5e]** where they are not in the vault.

### One-page overview: what actually changed

| Area | 3.5e | 5.5e / 2024 (SRD 5.2.1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core roll | d20 + modifiers, wide modifier range | d20 + modifier + Proficiency Bonus, **bounded accuracy** (AC tops out ~20) |
| Bonuses | Many stacking numeric bonuses | **Advantage / Disadvantage** (roll 2d20, take higher/lower) — usually *instead of* bonuses |
| Actions | Standard + move + swift + full-round + free | **One action, one Bonus Action (only if granted), one Reaction per round** |
| Skills | Rank-based (ranks + 3×class skill) with many sub-skills | 18 broad skills, **proficient or not** (+ PB), with Ability (Skill) checks |
| Feats | 100s of feats, chains, bonus feats at odd/even levels | **4 feat categories**, gained on a fixed level schedule (4/8/12/16…) |
| Ability scores | Racial +1/+2 or −2; level-up raises every 4 | Racial bonuses **gone**; **Background** grants +2/+1; level-up = Ability Score Improvement feat |
| Subclasses | Prestige classes, prestige levels | **Subclass at level 3** (one per class in the SRD), no prestige classes |
| Crits | Threat range + confirmation roll + ×2/×3/×4 | **Natural 20 only**, roll damage dice twice |
| Initiative/surprise | Roll Dexterity, flat-footed losers | Surprise = **Disadvantage on Initiative**; no flat-footed |
| Saving throws | 6 types, 2 good / 1 poor progression, many sub-types | **6 types**, proficiency-based (+ PB), 2 per class by fixed table |
| Spell slots | Spell per day per spell level, memorization | **Slots + prepared spells** (prepare, cast from slot), upcast |
| HP/dying | Negative HP → dead; stabilization roll | **0 HP → Unconscious + Death Saving Throws** (3 successes/failures) |
| Magic items | XP + gold + feat to craft; no attunement | **Attunement (max 3)**, rarity tiers, gold + time crafting (no XP) |
| Level cap | 20 + epic 21–30 | **20**, then bonus feats for XP beyond 355,000 |

The three tables below are the ones you will actually use at the table. Everything after this section is the detail behind them.

### Table A — Character rebuild, step by step

| Step | 3.5e character has… | Convert to 5.5e by… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Class + level | Same class + same level (see [[#Classes]]) |

## Ability Scores

### The 5.5e rules

> [!src] SRD pp. 21–22 — Step 3: Ability Scores
> Three generation methods: **Standard Array** (15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8), **Random Generation** (4d6 keep highest 3, six times), or **Point Cost** (27 points; 15 costs 9, 14 costs 7, 13 costs 5, 12 costs 4, 11 costs 3, 10 costs 2, 9 costs 1, 8 costs 0). Then **Adjust Ability Scores** per your background: increase one listed ability by 2 and another by 1 (or all three by 1); **no increase can raise a score above 20**.

Key differences from 3.5e:

1. **No racial ability modifiers.** Species (races) give traits, not numbers. The +2/+1 you used to get from your race now comes from your **Background**.
2. **Background is a rules object, not fluff.** It fixes three abilities you *must* boost, grants one Origin feat, two skills, one tool, and 50 GP of equipment (SRD p. 192, "Creating a Background").
3. **No rolling for ability damage/penalties** in the core loop; ability score increases come from the Ability Score Improvement feat and magic items.
4. The **Standard Array by Class** table (SRD p. 21) is the fastest conversion tool: it tells you where to put your six numbers for each class.

### Standard Array by Class (SRD p. 21) — use this to place scores

| Class | Str | Dex | Con | Int | Wis | Cha |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barbarian | 15 | 13 | 14 | 10 | 12 | 8 |
| Bard | 8 | 14 | 12 | 13 | 10 | 15 |
| Cleric | 14 | 8 | 13 | 10 | 15 | 12 |
| Druid | 8 | 12 | 14 | 13 | 15 | 10 |
| Fighter | 15 | 14 | 13 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
| Monk | 12 | 15 | 13 | 10 | 14 | 8 |
| Paladin | 15 | 10 | 13 | 8 | 12 | 14 |
| Ranger | 12 | 15 | 13 | 8 | 14 | 10 |
| Rogue | 12 | 15 | 13 | 14 | 10 | 8 |

| Warlock | 8 | 14 | 13 | 12 | 10 | 15 |
| Wizard | 8 | 12 | 13 | 15 | 14 | 10 |

### Conversion procedure for ability scores

1. **If your 3.5e scores are already roughly array-like** (highest ~15–16, lowest ~8), keep them. 3.5e's standard point buy (25 points) and 5.5e's (27 points) are close enough that a 3.5e character usually lands *within budget* — but check that the total point cost doesn't exceed 27 using the table above.
2. **If your scores are inflated** (3.5e rolling + racial boosts + inherent bonuses often produced an array like 18/16/14/12/10/8), **rebuild to the standard array or 27-point buy.** Bounded accuracy means a +1 here changes hit rates far more than it did in 3.5e.
3. **Move racial modifiers into your Background.** Example: a 3.5e Dwarf with +2 Con, +2 Wis (stonecunning) becomes a 5.5e Dwarf whose Background boosts Con and Wis. If your chosen Background's three abilities don't match your character, use the custom background rules (SRD p. 192) — you may pick any three abilities.
4. **Apply +2/+1 last**, and never exceed 20.
5. **Hit Points:** both systems add your Constitution modifier every level. In 5.5e you either roll the Hit Die + Con or take the **Fixed Hit Points by Class** value + Con (Barbarian 7, Fighter/Paladin/Ranger 6, Bard/Cleric/Druid/Monk/Rogue/Warlock 5, Sorcerer/Wizard 4 — SRD p. 23). What actually changed is the **absence of a negative-HP buffer**: at 0 you are Unconscious and making Death Saving Throws (SRD pp. 17–18), where 3.5e let you linger down to −10. Constitution is therefore worth more than it looked in 3.5e — keep it high.

> [!tip] Ability score increases at higher levels
> Do not port 3.5e's "+1 to an ability every 4 levels" as a standalone rule. In 5.5e it is the **Ability Score Improvement General feat** (Prerequisite: Level 4+), which grants +2 to one score or +1 to two scores (SRD p. 87). You may instead take *any* General feat or a Fighting Style feat on those levels — that is the 5.5e equivalent of your 3.5e bonus feats.

## Skills

### The 5.5e skill list (SRD p. 8) — 18 skills, full stop

| Skill | Ability | 3.5e skills it subsumes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acrobatics | Dexterity | Balance, Tumble, Use Rope (partial) |
| Animal Handling | Wisdom | Handle Animal |
| Arcana | Intelligence | Knowledge (arcana), Spellcraft (partial) |
| Athletics | Strength | Climb, Jump, Swim, (Escape Artist partial) |
| Deception | Charisma | Bluff, Disguise (partial) |
| History | Intelligence | Knowledge (history, geography, local, nobility) |
| Insight | Wisdom | Sense Motive |
| Intimidation | Charisma | Intimidate |
| Investigation | Intelligence | Search (partial), Appraise (partial) |
| Medicine | Wisdom | Heal |
| Nature | Intelligence | Knowledge (nature, geography, local) |
| Perception | Wisdom | **Spot + Listen** (merged) |
| Performance | Charisma | Perform |
| Persuasion | Charisma | Diplomacy |
| Religion | Intelligence | Knowledge (religion, planes) |
| Sleight of Hand | Dexterity | Sleight of Hand, Disable Device (partial) |
| Stealth | Dexterity | **Hide + Move Silently** (merged); Disable Device folds into Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) and Intelligence (Investigation) |
| Survival | Wisdom | Survival, Know Direction, some Track |

> [!important] The two famous merges
> - **Spot + Listen → Perception (Wisdom).** One proficiency covers both. Passive Perception = 10 + your Wisdom (Perception) modifier (SRD p. 21).
> - **Hide + Move Silently → Stealth (Dexterity).** One proficiency covers both.

### Structural changes

- **No ranks.** You are either proficient (+ PB) or you aren't. There is no "half-level cross-class skill" — a non-proficient character simply rolls the ability modifier, and many tasks are gated behind proficiency entirely.
- **No skill points, no class skill vs. cross-class distinction, no taking 10/take 20.** Taking 10 is replaced by **Passive** scores (Passive Perception is the only one defined in the SRD).
- **Tool proficiencies are separate** from skills (Thieves' Tools, artisan's tools, musical instruments — SRD pp. 99–101).
- **Expertise** (Bard level 2, Rogue level 1) doubles your proficiency bonus for specific skills — the direct descendant of 3.5e's "skill points in one class skill" specialization (SRD pp. 31, 63).

### Conversion procedure for skills

1. **Map each 3.5e skill to a 5.5e skill** using the table above. Two 3.5e skills mapping to one 5.5e skill collapse into a single proficiency.
2. **Choose only your class's listed proficiencies** (see the class table in [[#Classes]]). You cannot "buy" extra proficiencies with leftover skill points — use the **Skilled** Origin feat (+3 skills or tools, repeatable; SRD p. 88) or the Background's 2 skills.
3. **Discard all numeric skill bonuses.** The converted number is `ability modifier + PB` (if proficient) + any advantage/disadvantage from circumstance.
4. **Ask what the check is for.** 5.5e expects the GM to call for the check as `Ability (Skill)`; if your 3.5e build relied on a skill with no 5.5e analogue (e.g. Decipher Script, Disable Device as its own skill), use **Intelligence (Investigation)**, **Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)**, or the **Utilize action** for objects (SRD pp. 9–10).

> [!example] Worked example
> 3.5e Rogue 7 with ranks in Hide 11, Move Silently 11, Open Lock 11, Disable Device 11, Search 7, Spot 11, Listen 11, Bluff 7, Tumble 7 → 5.5e Rogue: choose 4 proficiencies from {Acrobatics, Athletics, Deception, Insight, Intimidation, Investigation, Perception, Persuasion, Sleight of Hand, Stealth}. Recommended: **Stealth, Acrobatics, Perception, Sleight of Hand** (locks/traps are handled by Thieves' Tools + Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) or Intelligence (Investigation), SRD p. 63).

## Feats

### How feats work in 5.5e

> [!src] SRD pp. 87–88 — Feats
> Every feat has a **Category** (Origin, General, Fighting Style, Epic Boon), an optional **Prerequisite**, a **Benefit**, and is **Repeatable** only if stated. Categories matter: a feature that says "choose a feat from the Origin category" means *only* Origin feats.

**Where feats come from (SRD pp. 23, 27):**

| Source | When |
| --- | --- |
| Background | Level 1: one **Origin** feat |
| Ability Score Improvement | Levels 4, 8, 12, 16 (plus 6 and 14 for Fighters; 10 for Rogues) — may be the ASI feat *or* any General/Fighting Style feat you qualify for |
| Class features | Fighting Style feats (Fighter 1, Paladin 2, Ranger 2, etc.); Expertise (Bard 2/Rogue 1) |
| Level 19 | **Epic Boon** feat (mandatory for all classes) |
| Level 20+ (beyond XP cap) | One bonus feat per 30,000 XP above 355,000 XP |

**The complete SRD 5.2.1 feat list (all feats in the SRD):**

| Category | Feats |
| --- | --- |
| **Origin** | Alert, Magic Initiate, Savage Attacker, Skilled |
| **General** | Ability Score Improvement, Grappler |
| **Fighting Style** | Archery, Defense, Great Weapon Fighting, Two-Weapon Fighting |
| **Epic Boon** | Boon of Combat Prowess, Boon of Dimensional Travel, Boon of Fate, Boon of Irresistible Offense, Boon of Night Spirit, Boon of Spell Recall, Boon of Truesight |

### Feat-by-feat conversion from 3.5e

| 3.5e feat (or category) | 5.5e conversion | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon Focus / Greater Weapon Focus | **No SRD equivalent.** Use Weapon Mastery (see below) + weapon choice | |
| Weapon Specialization / Greater Specialization | No SRD equivalent; class damage features (e.g. Fighter's Second-Strike style features) | |
| Improved Critical | **Champion subclass: Improved Critical** (19–20, level 3); Superior Critical (18–20) at 15 | SRD p. 49 |
| Power Attack / Cleave / Great Cleave | Weapon Mastery properties **Cleave, Graze, Vex, Nick, Sap, Topple** | SRD p. 90; Cleave triggers on a different target after a hit |
| Combat Expertise / Improved Disarm / Improved Trip | **Weapon mastery "Topple"**, and the Unarmed Strike options (Damage, Grapple, Shove) | SRD pp. 10, 180 |
| Improved Grapple / Improved Unarmed Strike | **Grappler** General feat + Unarmed Strike being a standard attack | SRD p. 87 |
| Dodge / Mobility | No direct feat; **Defense** Fighting Style (+1 AC) and the **Disengage** action | SRD pp. 9–10 |
| Two-Weapon Fighting (feat) | **Two-Weapon Fighting** Fighting Style feat + the built-in Light weapon property | SRD p. 88 |
| Rapid Reload / Manyshot / Crossbow feats | **Loading** and **Ammunition** weapon properties; no SRD feat | Crossbow Expert etc. are outside the SRD **[GAP]** |
| Exotic Weapon Proficiency | **No exotic weapons** exist in SRD 5.2.1 — all weapons are Simple or Martial | SRD pp. 90–96 |

| Tough | **Dwarven Toughness** (Dwarf only) or a homebrew General feat | General "Tough" is outside the SRD **[GAP]** |
| Spell Focus / Greater Spell Focus | Wizard subclass **Evocation Savant** (Evoker) narrows prep to evocation | SRD p. 82; no general Spell Focus feat |
| Spell Penetration / Improved Spell Resistance | **No Spell Resistance rule in SRD 5.2.1** | **[GAP]** |
| Craft Wondrous Item / Craft Magic Arms and Armor | **Crafting Magic Items** rules (proficiency + tool, gold + time, no XP) | SRD pp. 206–207 |
| Empower / Maximize / Extend Spell (metamagic feats) | **Metamagic** options on the Sorcerer class | Careful, Extended, Heightened, Quickened, Subtle, Twinned, Seeking, Transmuted — SRD pp. 70–71 |
| Leadership / Obtain familiar / Scribe scroll | No general feats; class features (Wizard's Ritual Familiar, Sorcerer's Innate Sorcery) | **[GAP]** for non-casters |
| Feat chains (3+ feats deep) | **Impossible.** A level 20 character in 5.5e has roughly **7–9 feats total** (Origin + ASIs + class feats + Epic Boon) | You must **prioritize**: pick the 3 benefits your build actually uses |
| Fighter bonus feats (every even level) | Fighter's Ability Score Improvement schedule is **4, 6, 8, 12, 14, 16** + Epic Boon at 19 | SRD p. 48 |
| Human bonus feat | Human's **Versatile** trait grants one **Origin** feat (not any feat) | SRD p. 86 |

> [!warning] The feat economy is the biggest nerf in conversion
> A level 12 3.5e Fighter with 6+ feats cannot become a level 12 5.5e Fighter with 6+ feats. Budget: Origin feat (1) + ASI feats at 4, 8, 12 (3) = **4 feats total**, and at least one of those three is likely spent on +2 ability scores. Decide which *one or two* mechanical benefits defined your character and rebuild around them.

### Weapon Mastery — the new system that replaces several feats

> [!src] SRD p. 90
> **Weapon Mastery** is a class feature (Barbarian, Fighter, Paladin, Ranger, Rogue at level 1). When you finish a Long Rest you choose mastery properties for the weapons you're proficient with and adopt them until you finish your next Long Rest. Mastery properties include: **Cleave, Graze, Nick, Push, Sap, Slow, Topple, Vex** (plus weapon properties like Light, Finesse, Heavy, Reach, Thrown, Two-Handed, Versatile, Loading, Ammunition, Range).

Conversion effect: 3.5e's "feat + specialization + weapon grouping" investment collapses into **pick mastery properties on a long rest.** A character who switched weapon styles in 3.5e by having several Weapon Focus feats should be built in 5.5e as a character whose mastery property choices change with the mission.

## Classes

### What stays and what changes

The twelve classes of 3.5e (Bard, Cleric, Druid, Fighter, Monk, Paladin, Ranger, Rogue, Sorcerer, Wizard, plus Barbarian and Warlock as 3.5e-era classes) all exist in 5.5e **with the same names and the same core fantasy**. The differences:

1. **Saving throws are fixed by class**, not by "good/poor" progression. Every class gets exactly two save proficiencies (table below).
2. **Every class has a distinct level-1 feature set** (Rage, Second Wind, Spellcasting, Martial Arts, Sneak Attack, Pact Magic, etc.) — 5.5e moved many features earlier: Paladins and Rangers now cast from level 1, Fighters get Fighting Style at level 1, subclasses arrive at level 3 for **all** classes (see [[#Subclasses]]).
3. **Spellcasting is rebuilt**: prepared casters prepare from a list (no more "spell known + per-day memorization" split), and every class has a **Prepared Spells** and **Spell Slots** table.
4. **No more bonus skill points, no more class skill caps, no more (½ BAB / ¾ BAB / full BAB)** — replaced by Proficiency Bonus.

> [!note] Warlock is special
> Pact Magic slots are **not** combined with other spellcasters' slots (SRD p. 25: only Bard/Cleric/Druid/Sorcerer/Wizard levels add fully, Paladin/Ranger count half, and Warlock's Pact Magic is handled separately). A 5.5e Warlock who multiclasses keeps two separate slot pools.

### Spellcasting conversion

> [!src] SRD pp. 104–105, 176–177 (Rules Glossary)
> Full casters (Bard, Cleric, Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard) prepare a number of spells equal to their **Prepared Spells** column plus their spellcasting ability modifier, and have a **Spell Slots** column for levels 1–9. Half casters (Paladin, Ranger) prepare from levels 1–5 and reach 5th-level slots at 17. Warlock uses **Pact Magic**: few slots, refreshed on a Short Rest.

| 3.5e concept | 5.5e equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| "Knows 4 spells at level 1, casts each once per day" | **Prepared Spells** (swap on a long rest) + **Spell Slots** (pooled) |
| Spell save DC = 10 + spell level + ability | **8 + Proficiency Bonus + ability** (SRD p. 7) |
| Spell Resistance / spell penetration | **[GAP]** — not in SRD 5.2.1 |
| Metamagic feats | Sorcerer **Metamagic** class feature (SRD pp. 70–71) |
| "Casting time: standard action" | "As an **Action**" (some are Bonus Action / Reaction / longer) |
| "Spell failure chance in armor" | Removed — armor only affects Stealth and certain Dexterity-based checks |
| Prepared divine casters praying for slots | Same idea, now a written **Prepared Spells** list you can change on a long rest |
| Familiar as a feat/summon | **Ritual Familiar** (Wizard), Pact of the Chain–style features are via Invocations (Warlock) |
| Spell school specialization (Wizard) | Wizard **subclass** (Evoker = Evocation Savant, SRD p. 82) |
| Concentration | Still exists — one Concentration spell at a time; Constitution saves to maintain |
| Spell lists by level | SRD pp. 104–175 (Spells section) — **reference these; do not reproduce them here** |

> [!tip] Converting a spellbook
> Walk your 3.5e spellbook spell by spell against the SRD spell list (SRD pp. 110–175). Many names are identical but the *effects changed* (e.g. *Fireball* is still 8d6 in the SRD; *Polymorph* is rebuilt; *Scrying* is renamed/rewritten). Where a spell has no SRD equivalent, pick the closest SRD spell of the same level and note the substitution in your character's "conversion notes."

### Multiclass spell slots (SRD p. 25)

Add: **all** Bard/Cleric/Druid/Sorcerer/Wizard levels + **half** (round up) of Paladin/Ranger levels. Look up that total in the Multiclass Spellcaster table for your shared slot pool. Prepare spells per class separately, using each class's own ability modifier.

### Conversion procedure for races

1. **Map your 3.5e race to the closest 5.5e species.** Common mappings: Human→Human; Elf/Drow/High Elf/Wood Elf→Elf (+ lineage); Hill/Mountain/Deep Dwarf→Dwarf; Rock/Gnome→Gnome (+ lineage); Halfling/Halfling (Stout)→Halfling; Dragonborn→Dragonborn; Tiefling→Tiefling (choose legacy); Half-Orc→**Orc**; Half-Elf→**Human** (Versatile) or Elf, per table flavor; Goliath→Goliath; Gnome→Gnome.
2. **Delete racial ability score modifiers** and move them into your Background's three abilities (see [[#Ability Scores]]).
3. **Re-express racial Feats** (e.g. Skill Focus (Elf), Alertness (Half-Elf)) as **Skilled** or class skill proficiencies.
4. **Darkvision:** 60 ft standard (Dwarf/Orc get 120; Halfling/Gnome/Dragonborn/Human/Goliath get none in the SRD — Gnome's *does* exist: 60 ft).
5. **Level 0 / racial HD / monstrous races:** the SRD species list has **no monstrous playable species** (no Tiefling-adjacent exotic races beyond the nine, no incorporeal, no size Large at level 1). A 3.5e character with a monstrous template needs GM approval or an improvised species (custom background + GM-granted traits) **[GAP]**.

### Backgrounds (SRD pp. 83, 192)

The SRD gives four sample backgrounds — **Acolyte** (Insight, Religion), **Criminal** (Sleight of Hand, Stealth), **Sage** (Arcana, History), **Soldier** (Athletics, Intimidation) — plus full custom-background rules (SRD p. 192: choose 3 abilities → +2/+1 or +1/+1/+1 (max 20), 1 Origin feat, 2 skills, 1 tool, 50 GP equipment package).

> [!tip] The cleanest race→background trick
> When a 3.5e racial bonus doesn't fit the sample backgrounds, **write a custom background** (SRD p. 192) that matches your character's race-flavored origin: choose the three abilities your old racial modifiers went to, an Origin feat that echoes your old racial feat, and two skills from your class list.

### Conversion procedure for subclasses and prestige classes

1. **If your 3.5e character had an in-class archetype/subclass** (e.g. 3.5e *Draconic* bloodline sorcerer, *Whirlwind* fighter variant) → pick the matching SRD subclass.
2. **If your 3.5e character had a prestige class**, choose one of three strategies (see [[#Multiclassing, Prestige Classes, and Epic Play]]).
3. **If your 3.5e subclass concept has no SRD subclass** (Assassin, Evocation-spec Wizard variants, Wild Magic, Berserker variants, Arcane Trickster, Eldritch Knight, Battlemaster), you have three options:
   - Take the closest SRD subclass and re-skin it (best default).
   - **Build it with feats**: e.g. Arcane Trickster ≈ Rogue (Thief) + **Magic Initiate** + Skilled; Eldritch Knight ≈ Fighter (Champion) + **Magic Initiate**.
   - Homebrew a subclass with your GM, following the level-3/7/11/17 feature cadence visible in the SRD subclass tables.
4. **Subclass features are fixed to class level**, not to prestige level. A 10th-level 3.5e character who took 5 levels of prestige class becomes a 10th-level 5.5e character of their base class with a subclass feature at level 7 and level 11 (whatever their class table grants).

## Multiclassing, Prestige Classes, and Epic Play

### Multiclassing (SRD pp. 24–25)

> [!src] SRD p. 24 — Prerequisites
> "To qualify for a new class, you must have a score of at least **13 in the primary ability of the new class and your current classes**." (Example given: Barbarian→Druid needs Strength *and* Wisdom 13+.)

Key rules:

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| XP | Based on **total character level**, not per-class level |
| Hit Points | Level-1 Hit Die value only at character level 1; otherwise the new class's per-level die |
| Hit Dice | Pooled (same die types stack) |
| Proficiency Bonus | Based on **total character level** |
| Starting proficiencies | You gain **only some** of the new class's starting proficiencies (see each class's "As a Multiclass Character" block) |
| Armor class features | Only **one** base AC calculation may be active (Unarmored Defense vs. Draconic Resilience, etc.) |
| Extra Attack | Does not stack across classes |
| Spellcasting | Full casters add, half casters count half, Warlock Pact Magic stays separate (SRD p. 25) |

### Prestige classes — no direct equivalent

3.5e prestige classes (Arcane Trickster, Assassin, Blackguard, Dragon Disciple, Eldritch Knight, Loremaster, Shadowdancer, etc.) have **no rule in 5.5e**. Conversion strategies, in order of preference:

1. **Subclass re-skin.** *Dragon Disciple* → Sorcerer (**Draconic Sorcery**). *Assassin* → Rogue (**Thief**) with a poison kit, or note that the Assassin subclass exists in the 2024 *PHB* but **not in the SRD [GAP]**. *Shadowdancer* → Monk (Warrior of the Open Hand) or Rogue with Magic Initiate.
2. **Multiclass.** *Arcane Trickster* → Rogue 3 / Wizard 3 (this works cleanly under the 13-in-both-primary-abilities rule: Dexterity 13+, Intelligence 13+). *Eldritch Knight* → Fighter / Wizard. *Paladin-of-the-Undead / Blackguard* → Paladin + Warlock (both Charisma-primary, so prerequisites are trivial).
3. **Feats as a stand-in.** *Arcane Devotee / Divine Champion* → **Magic Initiate** + Skilled, staying single-classed.
4. **Convert the flavor, not the progression.** Prestige classes were usually gated by alignment, skill ranks, and feat prerequisites. 5.5e gates by level 3 subclass + level 4 feat choices.

> [!warning] The 13/13 prerequisite is a real filter
> A 3.5e Dwarf Fighter 6 / Rage Mage 4 (a Wisdom-gated prestige class) may **fail** the 5.5e multiclass prerequisite if Wisdom is below 13. Options: adjust the ability array during conversion (recommended), or stay single-classed and re-skin.

### Epic levels (21–30)

> [!src] SRD p. 23 — Character Advancement
> The Character Advancement table stops at **20** (355,000 XP). Level 19 grants every class an **Epic Boon** feat. Level 20 is a capstone feature per class. Beyond the table, each additional **30,000 XP above 355,000** grants a bonus feat (Epic Boon feats are appropriate).

Conversion of an epic 3.5e character (levels 21–30):

1. Build the character at level 20.
2. For each level above 20, add an **Epic Boon** feat (Boon of Combat Prowess, Dimensional Travel, Fate, Irresistible Offense, Night Spirit, Spell Recall, Truesight are the SRD options).
3. **Epic spellcasting** (3.5e's 10th–12th level spells, epic spell seeds, material component costs) has **no SRD equivalent [GAP]**. Practical answers: use 9th-level spells with stronger descriptions, or let a high-level *wish*-style effect cover it with GM adjudication.
4. **Epic Save DC / epic spell resistance / automatic failure on natural 1 for epic characters** — not in SRD 5.2.1 **[GAP]**.
5. Consider capping epic play at **level 20 + a handful of Epic Boons**, which is the design intent of the 2024 rules.

### XP comparison (conversion by level, not by XP)

| Level | 3.5e XP **[3.5e]** | 5.5e XP (SRD p. 23) | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 1,000 | 300 | 5.5e is faster at low levels |
| 5 | 10,000 | 6,500 | |
| 10 | 45,000 | 64,000 | 5.5e is slower from ~9th on |
| 15 | 105,000 | 195,000 | |
| 20 | 190,000 | 355,000 | |

> [!tip] Recommendation
> **Never convert by XP total.** Convert by **level**: a 3.5e 10th-level character becomes a 5.5e 10th-level character, regardless of XP. The curves diverge enough that matching XP would produce wildly wrong levels.

## Combat and the Action Economy

### The action economy (SRD pp. 9–10, 16)

> [!src] SRD pp. 9–10 — Actions
> On your turn you take **one action** (from: Attack, Cast a Spell, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, Influence, Ready, Search, Study, Utilize), move up to your Speed, and — **only if a feature grants it** — take **one Bonus Action**. You get **one Reaction** per round (it can trigger before or after your turn, and resets at the start of your next turn). You can also speak briefly and interact with one object for free during your movement.

#### 3.5e → 5.5e action mapping

| 3.5e **[3.5e]** | 5.5e equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| Standard action: attack, cast, etc. | **Action** (Attack, Cast a Spell, Help, Hide, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Ready, Search, Study, Utilize, Influence) |
| Move action | Your **movement** (up to Speed), taken any time during your turn |
| Swift action | **Bonus Action** — but *only if a feature explicitly grants one* (there is no "free swift action every round") |
| Free action | **Free object interaction** (one per turn) or brief speech; more requires the **Utilize** action |
| Full-round action (full attack, cast, charge) | The **Attack action** (all your attacks), or **Cast a Spell**, or **Dash** |
| Immediate action | **Reaction** (one per round) |
| Five-foot step | **Removed.** Moving out of an enemy's reach provokes unless you **Disengage** (which occupies your whole action) |
| Partial action / full attack at penalty | Not applicable — every attack in the Attack action uses the same d20 with no iterative −5 penalties |
| Aid another | **Help** action |
| Concentration (spell) | **Concentration** (one spell at a time; Constitution saving throw when you take damage) |

### Attack rolls, damage, and critical hits

| Topic | 3.5e **[3.5e]** | 5.5e (SRD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attack roll | d20 + STR/DEX + BAB + size + misc. | d20 + ability modifier + **Proficiency Bonus** (+ magic weapon bonus) |
| Critical | Roll a natural within threat range → confirm with a second attack roll → damage ×2/×3/×4 | **Natural 20 only.** No confirmation roll. **Roll all damage dice twice** and add modifiers (SRD p. 179) |
| Expanded crit range | Improved Critical / keen | Only via a feature (Champion's Improved Critical: 19–20; Superior Critical: 18–20) |
| Natural 1 on an attack | Automatic miss | Automatic **miss** (SRD p. 7) |
| Natural 1/20 on saves & checks | Common house rule = auto fail/success | **Not a rule in SRD 5.2.1** — the nat 1/nat 20 rule is defined only for **attack rolls** (SRD p. 7) and separately for **Death Saving Throws** (nat 1 = two failures, nat 20 = regain 1 HP, SRD p. 18) |
| Damage resistance/vulnerability | Multiplicative (×½, ×2) with flat reductions | Applied **in order**: flat adjustments → Resistance (halve) → Vulnerability (double) (SRD p. 17) |
| Two-weapon fighting | Penalties, extra attack with off-hand | Two **Light** weapons; a feature (Two-Weapon Fighting style, Nick property) may grant the extra attack |
| Ranged in melee | Provokes AoO | **Disadvantage** on the attack roll (no AoO) — SRD p. 13 |
| Unarmed strike | d20 + STR, 1d3 + STR | **Unarmed Strike** attack option (Damage, Grapple, or Shove) — SRD p. 10 |

### Opportunity Attacks (SRD pp. 14–15)

> [!src] SRD pp. 14–15
> You can make **one Opportunity Attack when a creature you can see leaves your reach** by voluntarily moving (not teleporting, and not being pushed). It uses your **Reaction**, and you make it *before* the creature moves away. You **don't** provoke if the creature is forced to move, teleports, or spends the Disengage action. Only melee weapon attacks and Unarmed Strikes (not spells with a range) can be used for it.

| 3.5e trigger **[3.5e]** | 5.5e |
| --- | --- |
| Moving out of a threatened square | ✅ Yes — leaving a creature's reach |
| Casting a spell in melee | ❌ No — replaced by **Concentration** and possibly Disadvantage |
| Ranged attack in melee | ❌ No — replaced by **Disadvantage** |
| Leaving a square without 5-ft. step | ✅ Same as above |
| Throwing/picking up items, etc. | ❌ No |
| Free action / immediate action AoO | ❌ No — exactly one Reaction |

> [!important] The single biggest combat change
> In 3.5e, **standing still and full-attacking was usually optimal**, and movement was free-ish. In 5.5e, **movement and positioning matter**: you must spend your action (Disengage) to escape without provoking, and enemies will use their Reaction to hit you when you flee. Convert melee "brick wall" characters into **opportunity-attack denial** builds (Disengage features, reach weapons, the Mobile-equivalent tactics your GM allows).

### Things that are gone or changed — detailed

#### Flanking
> [!gap] **[GAP]** Flanking is **not** in SRD 5.2.1 (verified: the term does not appear).
> 5.5e core uses **Advantage/Disadvantage** instead of numeric position bonuses. If your table wants flanking, the standard optional rule (2024 *DMG*, outside this vault) grants **Advantage on attack rolls** when you and an ally are on opposite sides of a creature. Until your GM adopts a rule, convert 3.5e flanking into **the Help action** (SRD p. 10) or a Rogue's Sneak Attack conditions (which already key off "an ally is within 5 feet of the target" — SRD p. 63).

#### Charge
> [!gap] **[GAP]** There is **no Charge action** in SRD 5.2.1 (verified: the term does not appear).
> Conversion options:
> 1. **Dash + Attack** — use your action to Dash, then your remaining movement to close; this is the RAW approximation (you lose the +2 attack bonus and the 5.5e version has no "double move then attack" trade-off).
> 2. **Orc's Adrenaline Rush / Monk's Unarmored Movement / Cunning Action** style features (Bonus Action Dash) for characters whose identity was "I charge."
> 3. Ask your GM for a house rule: "When you move in a straight line at least 10 feet toward a creature and then hit it with a melee attack on the same turn, you have Advantage on the attack roll; you can't do this if you used Disengage" — this is the closest analogue to 3.5e's Charge (which granted +2 to hit, −2 AC, and required a straight-line double move).

#### Full-round actions → the Attack action
A 3.5e **full attack** (BAB +6/+1) becomes the **Attack action** at level 5+ with **Extra Attack** (SRD p. 48): you make two attacks with the same bonus. A level 11 Fighter makes three ("Two Extra Attacks"). **There is no iterative penalty** — a 3.5e +15/+10/+5 routine becomes +8/+8/+8 in 5.5e math, which is why **bounded accuracy** matters: do not try to reproduce 3.5e's escalating attack bonuses.

#### Surprise, initiative, and the first round
- 3.5e Surprise Round: flat-footed characters lose their action; initiative = d20 + Dex (+Improved Initiative).
- 5.5e: a creature that is **Surprised** has **Disadvantage on its Initiative roll** and can't take a Reaction until after its first turn (SRD p. 16, per the 5.1→5.2.1 change list in [[converting-to-srd-5.2.1]]). There is **no flat-footed condition**; instead, the **Hide** action and **Invisible** condition replace most of it. **Alert** (Origin feat) adds PB to Initiative (SRD p. 87).

#### Movement and speed
- 5-ft. squares and Speed in feet are unchanged.
- Difficult terrain, climbing, jumping, and swimming exist as glossary rules (SRD p. 180) — jump distance is no longer a Strength check + 10 ft. formula; it is a fixed base with modifiers (check "Jumping" in the SRD glossary).
- **50% speed** for medium/heavy armor in 3.5e is gone — armor only imposes Disadvantage on Stealth (and blocks it entirely for Heavy armor wearers).

#### Mounted combat, vehicles, and underwater combat
Mounted combat rules exist in the SRD (SRD p. 15: controlling a mount, fighting while mounted), plus *Find Steed* (Paladin, SRD p. 57) and mount stat blocks. Underwater combat is **[GAP]** (not detailed in SRD 5.2.1).

### Death, dying, and stabilization (SRD pp. 17–18)

| 3.5e **[3.5e]** | 5.5e (SRD) |
| --- | --- |
| Drop to 0 HP → **dying** (−1 HP, each round roll 1d20, fail = move 1 HP closer to −10) | Reach 0 HP → **Unconscious** + **Death Saving Throws**: roll d20, 10+ = success; **3 successes = Stable**, **3 failures = die** (SRD p. 17) |
| Natural 20 on a death save = −1 HP (conscious but disabled) | Natural 20 on a death save = **regain 1 HP** (awake); natural 1 = **two failures** (SRD p. 18) |
| Stabilize: DC 15 Heal check (first aid) | Stabilize: **Help** action, **DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine)** check; a stable creature regains 1 HP after 1d4 hours (SRD p. 18) |
| Negative HP, ability damage on death | **Massive Damage**: if damage at 0 HP equals/exceeds your HP maximum, you die (SRD p. 17) |
| Killing a helpless foe | **Knocking Out**: a melee attack that drops a creature to 0 HP can leave it at 1 HP + Unconscious (it then starts a Short Rest) (SRD p. 17) |

> [!tip] Converting a "death's door" house rule
> If your 3.5e table used "we play until −10," adopt 5.5e's three-failure track as-is — it produces similar tension with far less bookkeeping.

| Topic | 3.5e **[3.5e]** | 5.5e (SRD) |

| Armor penalty to skills | Armor check penalty on many skills | **Only Stealth** (Disadvantage, or automatic failure with Heavy armor) — SRD pp. 89–91 |

| Armor training restrictions by class | Weapon/armor proficiency lists | **Armor Training** field per class (Light/Medium/Heavy/Shields) — see [[#Class reference table (SRD pp. 28–82)]] |
| Exotic weapons (bastard sword, double weapons, etc.) | A category | **No exotic weapons**; every weapon is Simple or Martial — SRD pp. 90–96 |
| Masterwork (+1 enhancement, non-magical) | +1 to attack only | No masterwork; a magic weapon's bonus applies to attack *and* damage |
| Weapon properties | Brace, Double, Monstrous, Thrown, 2H | **Cleave, Graze, Light, Finesse, Heavy, Nick, Push, Reach, Sap, Slow, Thrown, Topple, Two-Handed, Vex, Versatile, Loading, Ammunition, Range** |
| Special materials (mithral, adamantine) | Weight/stealth/DR benefits | Mithral/Adamantine exist as magic item properties; check the SRD Magic Items section (SRD pp. 204–253) |
| Stackable consumables | Potions/scrolls stack | Same — see Potions and Scrolls in the Magic Items section |

### Starting wealth and "wealth by level"

> [!src] SRD pp. 19–24, 89
> Level 1: each class has a **Starting Equipment** package (choose A or B), with GP costs: Barbarian 75, Bard 90, Cleric 110, Druid 50, Fighter 50, Monk 50, Paladin 150, Ranger 150, Rogue 100, Sorcerer 50, Warlock 100, Wizard 55. For higher-level starts (SRD p. 24):

| Starting level | Equipment and money | Magic items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2–4 | Normal starting equipment | 1 Common |
| 5–10 | 500 GP + 1d10 × 25 GP + normal starting equipment | 1 Common, 1 Uncommon |
| 11–16 | 5,000 GP + 1d10 × 250 GP + normal starting equipment | 2 Common, 3 Uncommon, 1 Rare |
| 17–20 | 20,000 GP + 1d10 × 250 GP + normal starting equipment | 2 Common, 4 Uncommon, 3 Rare, 1 Very Rare |

> [!tip] Converting a 3.5e WBL (wealth by level) character
> 3.5e's WBL tables (DMG) are **more generous at mid-level** than the table above, and 5.5e has no WBL table. Practical approach: **use the SRD "Starting Equipment at Higher Levels" table as your budget**, then keep the 3.5e character's magic items **if** their rarity fits the column (a level 11 character can keep 2 Common, 3 Uncommon, 1 Rare). Anything beyond that should be downgraded, sold, or granted by the GM.

### Magic items

| Topic | 3.5e **[3.5e]** | 5.5e (SRD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attunement | None (except a few items) | **Attunement: max 3 items at once**; requires a **Short Rest** while in contact; no two copies of the same item (SRD p. 204) |
| Rarity | No formal tiering | **Common / Uncommon / Rare / Very Rare / Legendary / Artifact** — SRD p. 205 |
| Selling loot | ~½ market value (full for trade goods) | Same principle: selling equipment brings **half** its cost; trade goods sell for full value (SRD p. 89) |
| Crafting | Feat + XP + gold + weeks | **Proficiency in Arcana (or the item's relevant skill) + a tool proficiency + gold + time**; no XP cost — SRD pp. 206–207 |
| Adding enhancements (+1 → +1, keen) | Gold + XP cost, sometimes a feat | Re-craft or buy a higher-rarity item |
| Cursed items | Common | Present (SRD pp. 193, e.g. cursed magic items) |
| Masterwork | Separate from magic | Folded into magic items |
| Component pouch / arcane focus / holy symbol | Varies | Still present (SRD pp. 96–101) |
| Potion of healing | d8+1 (mod) | Potion of Healing (heals a set amount, rarity Common) |

**Attunement quick rules (SRD p. 204):**

1. You can attune to **no more than three** magic items at once.
2. Attunement takes **a Short Rest** while you hold or wear the item (you can attune to two items in one short rest only if the item says so — normally one item per short rest by the standard rule).
3. You can't attune to **more than one copy** of the same item.
4. Finishing a **Long Rest** while you hold/wear the item maintains attunement; the GM can require an Intelligence (Arcana) check to identify an unidentified item.

> [!warning] Rebuild your magic item list, don't paste it
> A 3.5e level-10 character often carries 8–12 magic items. Rebuild against the 11–16 column: **2 Common, 3 Uncommon, 1 Rare**. Convert items with no SRD equivalent (e.g. *boots of speed* → *Boots of Speed* exists; *cloak of displacement* → check SRD; *ring of three wishes* → *Ring of Three Wishes* is Legendary) by rarity, and drop or downgrade the rest.

## Conversion Workflow: Step-by-Step

Use this as an ordered checklist when rebuilding a character sheet. The target format is the official 2024 character sheet ([[character-sheet]]), whose fields define the finished product: Ability Scores, Background, Species, Class/Subclass, Proficiencies, HP, AC, Attacks, Features & Traits, Spells, Equipment, **Attunement**, and **Heroic Inspiration**.

1. **Record the concept.** Name, class, level, subclass concept, party role, signature magic items, and the 2–3 mechanical "pillars" you refuse to lose (e.g. "crits on 19–20", "sneak attack", "I'm the party face").
2. **Write the 5.5e skeleton:** Class → Level → Subclass (level 3) → Species → Background.
3. **Set ability scores:** standard array placed per the Standard Array by Class table, or 27-point buy, with Background +2/+1 applied and nothing above 20.
4. **Proficiency Bonus** from the Character Advancement table (SRD p. 23).
5. **Saving throws:** take the class's two from [[#Class reference table (SRD pp. 28–82)]]. Delete all 3.5e save bonuses.
6. **Skills:** choose exactly the number your class lists, from the class list. Note expertise choices if Bard/Rogue.
7. **Tools, languages, proficiencies:** weapon proficiencies, armor training, tool proficiencies from the class table; languages from species (SRD p. 19 lists dialects/primordial).
8. **Feats:** Origin feat (from Background) → ASI/General/Fighting Style feats at your class's schedule → Epic Boon at 19. Prune hard (see [[#Feats]]).
9. **Combat stats:** HP (maximum of your Hit Die at level 1 + Con modifier, then roll or take the Fixed Hit Points value + Con each level — SRD p. 23), AC (10 + Dex + armor + shield + one feature), initiative, speed, attacks (ability + PB, weapon mastery property chosen).
10. **Features & Traits:** transcribe your class features level by level from the class Features table in the SRD (e.g. Paladin Features, SRD p. 53; Bard Features, SRD p. 31).
11. **Spells:** rebuild prepared/known from the SRD spell lists; assign slots per the class table; note Pact Magic separately for Warlocks.
12. **Equipment:** starting package or the higher-level table; assign rarities; **list attuned items (max 3)** in the Attunement box.
13. **Conversion notes box:** append a short list of every substitution you made (spell renames, homebrew flanking/charge rules your GM allows, dropped prestige features). Future-you will thank you.
14. **Playtest the math:** check that your attack bonus is `ability + PB + weapon bonus`, your AC is in the 14–19 band for your level, and your attack routine matches the class's Extra Attack progression — not 3.5e's iterative bonuses.

### Adventure / campaign conversion (not just characters)

| 3.5e element | 5.5e handling |
| --- | --- |
| CR-based XP awards | Use the 5.5e Monster CR→XP table (SRD Monster section) — CR is not redefined in the SRD, but 5.5e monsters are built differently |
| Monster stat blocks (3.5e format) | **Don't convert; replace.** Use an SRD monster of the same CR and rename/reskin. 5.5e monsters have legendary actions, lair actions, and different save math |
| DCs (3.5e: 10 + 5/10/15 by difficulty) | Map: Easy ≈ DC 8–10, Medium ≈ DC 12, Hard ≈ DC 15, Very Hard ≈ DC 18, Nearly Impossible ≈ DC 20 (5.5e's bounded range) |
| Traps with attack rolls and DCs | Keep DCs, convert saves to 5.5e six-ability saves, and use advantage/disadvantage instead of numeric modifiers |
| Spellcasting NPCs | Use the 5.5e stat blocks or a classed NPC block from the SRD |
| Encounter balance | 3.5e "party level +3 is deadly" doesn't hold; use 5.5e encounter math — **in the SRD**: XP Budget per Character, Low/Moderate/High difficulty, worked examples and troubleshooting (SRD pp. 202–203) |

## Known Gaps and Ambiguities

Everything below was **verified absent or unresolved** during extraction of [[SRD_CC_v5.2.1]]. Treat these as open questions for your GM rather than as "rules that must exist somewhere."

### Confirmed absent from SRD 5.2.1 (searched, not found)

1. **Flanking** — the word does not appear. Positional advantage is a GM/optional rule.
2. **Charge** — no Charge action, no charge bonuses/penalties.
3. **Spell Resistance and Spell Penetration** — no SRD mechanic for monsters resisting spells by spellcraft-style SR.
4. **Ability damage and ability drain** — not in the Rules Glossary; only the six ability scores themselves and their modifiers.
5. **Exotic weapons, masterwork weapons, double weapons, armor check penalty, arcane spell failure chance** — all removed from the equipment chapter.
6. **Prestige classes** — no rule text anywhere.
7. **Epic-level mechanics (21–30), epic feats, epic spellcasting** — the Character Advancement table stops at 20; only the "bonus feat per 30,000 XP" sentence extends play.
8. **Monstrous/underdark playable species beyond the nine listed**; no Incorporeal, no level-1 Large species.
9. **Saving throw sub-categories** such as "Fortitude/Reflex/Will" as distinct systems (they are Constitution/Dexterity/Wisdom saves now), and no "spell resistance" or "ability damage" saves.
10. **Prestige classes' alignment/skill-rank prerequisites**, NPC reputation rules, and the 3.5e DMG's organization rules.

### Present but under-specified in the SRD

1. **Encounter difficulty math** — *corrected 2026-09-27:* SRD 5.2.1 **does** contain encounter math: the **XP Budget per Character** table with Low/Moderate/High difficulties (SRD p. 202), worked examples, and a troubleshooting subsection (p. 203). What is still absent: a **daily/encountering XP budget**, a per-monster "CR vs. party level" difficulty chart, and random-encounter tables — those remain *DMG* territory.
2. **Only one subclass per class** — the other 2024 PHB subclasses (Assassin, Battlemaster, Evoker variants, Wild Magic, Arcane Trickster, Eldritch Knight, College of Valor, etc.) are **not in the SRD**; re-skin or homebrew.
3. **Only four sample backgrounds** (Acolyte, Criminal, Sage, Soldier) — all other 2024 backgrounds (Guild Artisan, Folk Hero, Charlatan…) are absent; use the custom background rules (SRD p. 192).
4. **Charisma (Leadership)**, followers, and retainers — no rules.
5. **Underwater, aerial, and mounted encounter-building guidance** — limited; mounted combat basics exist (SRD p. 15) but no full subsystem.
6. **Warlock Pact Magic slot count and Eldritch Invocation list** — present (SRD pp. 74–76) but the full invocation list is worth reading directly rather than summarizing here.
7. **Spell list reconciliation** — many 3.5e spells (e.g. *summon monster* variants, *shield other*, *spell turning*, *permanency*) have no obvious SRD 5.2.1 counterpart; each needs a manual mapping. There is no official 3.5e→5e spell conversion table in the SRD.
8. **Exhaustion's interaction with long rests for PCs** — the SRD describes the condition and rest recovery, but 3.5e's "you must rest 8 hours or remain fatigued" nuance needs the glossary text read in full (SRD p. 179).
9. **Concentration checks** — the SRD defines Concentration (Rules Glossary) but the 5.5e "take damage → DC 10 or half damage, whichever is higher" formula should be read in the glossary before first use; don't assume the 5e version is verbatim.

### Sources used for 3.5e-side facts (not in this vault)

The 3.5e half of every comparison table is drawn from the standard 3.5e rules: the *Player's Handbook* (ability scores, skills, feats, saves, BAB), the *Dungeon Master's Guide* (XP table, wealth by level, encounter difficulty), and the *Monster Manual* (CR/XP awards). Anything marked **[3.5e]** should be checked against your own 3.5e books before being treated as authoritative in a table dispute.
