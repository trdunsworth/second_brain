# Economy and Downtime

SRD 5.2.1 has **prices, crafting, and market access** — on SRD pp. 89, 93–103, 205–207 — and **no downtime system at all** (full-text search: the word `downtime` appears twice, both describing rests: SRD pp. 185, 187). What follows is the priced half in full, plus a way to build the missing half out of rules the SRD already has.

## The coin system and the one resale rule (SRD p. 89)

> [!src] SRD p. 89 — Coin Values
> | Coin | Value in GP |
> | --- | --- |
> | Copper Piece (CP) | 1/100 |
> | Silver Piece (SP) | 1/10 |
> | Electrum Piece (EP) | 1/2 |
> | Gold Piece (GP) | 1 |
> | Platinum Piece (PP) | 10 |
>
> "A coin weighs about a third of an ounce, so fifty coins weigh a pound" (SRD p. 89). That is a real encumbrance fact: 5,000 GP in coin is **100 pounds**.

> [!src] SRD p. 89 — Selling Equipment
> "Equipment fetches **half its cost** when sold. In contrast, **trade goods and valuables—like gems and art objects—retain their full value** in the marketplace. 'Magic Items' has prices for magic items."

Three consequences worth building on:

1. **Loot in the form of gear is a 50% tax.** A party that strips and sells armor loses half of everything — so towns that buy gear at half are a *system*, not a detail.
2. **Trade goods are the store of value.** If you want wealth that survives a market, put gems and art objects in the hoard, not plate mail.
3. **Magic items have their own price list** (SRD p. 206, below).

> [!warning] The other half of the resale rule
> The SRD gives **no** buy/sell price variance by region, no haggling rule, and no supply-and-demand rule. "Rarity can help you set prices for those items. Gold Piece values are provided… though **a seller might ask for a service rather than coin** as payment" (SRD p. 205). That last clause is your sanction for faction-shaped prices → [[Factions and Organizations Template]].

## Crafting — four ladders

### 1. Nonmagical items (SRD p. 103)

- **Tools:** you must use the required tool *and* have proficiency in it; helpers must too.
- **Raw materials:** worth **half the purchase cost, round down** — "you need 750 GP of raw materials to make Plate Armor, which sells for 1,500 GP."
- **Time:** purchase cost in GP **÷ 10**, round a fraction up to a day, 8 hours per day; days needn't be consecutive.
- **Assistants:** divide the time by the number working; normally one assistant.
- **Outcome:** "you make the item, and you can use it or sell it at its normal price."

> Worked example from the SRD: a Heavy Crossbow takes 5 days and "sells for 50 GP" (SRD p. 103).

### 2. Brewing Potions of Healing (SRD p. 103)

Herbalism Kit proficiency + kit + **25 GP of raw material**, **1 day** (8 hours).

### 3. Scribing Spell Scrolls (SRD p. 103)

| Spell Level | Time | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cantrip | 1 day | 15 GP |
| 1 | 1 day | 25 GP |
| 2 | 3 days | 100 GP |
| 3 | 5 days | 150 GP |
| 4 | 10 days | 1,000 GP |
| 5 | 25 days | 1,500 GP |
| 6 | 40 days | 10,000 GP |
| 7 | 50 days | 12,500 GP |
| 8 | 60 days | 15,000 GP |
| 9 | 120 days | 50,000 GP |

Prerequisites: **Arcana proficiency or Calligrapher's Supplies proficiency**, plus the spell **prepared on each day of inscription**, plus any Material components at hand. The scroll uses your spell save DC and attack bonus; a scribed cantrip "works as if the caster were your level" (SRD p. 103).

*Sanity check for GMs:* buying a 1st-level scroll costs 50 GP (SRD p. 99) while scribing one costs 25 GP and a day — crafters undercut the market by half. Price your town's market accordingly.

### 4. Magic items (SRD pp. 206–207)

> [!src] SRD p. 207 — Magic Item Crafting Time and Cost
> | Item Rarity | Time | Cost |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | Common | 5 days | 50 GP |
> | Uncommon | 10 days | 200 GP |
> | Rare | 50 days | 2,000 GP |
> | Very Rare | 125 days | 20,000 GP |
> | Legendary | 250 days | 100,000 GP |
>
> "The time and cost are halved for a consumable item other than a Spell Scroll."

Requirements: **Arcana proficiency** for you and any assistants; the right tool from the **Magic Item Tools** table (Potion → Alchemist's Supplies or Herbalism Kit; Scroll → Calligrapher's Supplies; Weapon/Armor → Leatherworker's, Smith's, or Weaver's; Wondrous → Tinker's); if the item casts spells, those spells **prepared every crafting day**; 8 hours per day.

**Raw materials availability:** "In a city, there is a **75 percent chance** that the materials are available, and in any other settlement, that chance is **25 percent**. If materials aren't available, you must wait at least **7 days** before checking… again" (SRD p. 207).

**If the item is built on a real item:** pay that item's whole cost or craft it — "to make +1 Armor (Plate Armor), you must pay 3,500 GP or pay 2,000 GP and craft the armor" (SRD p. 207).

## The magic-item market (SRD pp. 204–208)

> [!src] SRD p. 206 — Magic Item Rarities and Values
> | Rarity | Value | Rarity | Value |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | Common | 100 GP | Very Rare | 40,000 GP |
> | Uncommon | 400 GP | Legendary | 200,000 GP |
> | Rare | 4,000 GP | Artifact | Priceless |
>
> "**Halve the value for a consumable item other than a Spell Scroll. The value of a Spell Scroll is double what it costs to scribe the scroll.**"
>
> Worked example (SRD p. 205): +1 Plate Armor = 4,000 GP (Rare) + 1,500 GP (Plate Armor) = **5,500 GP**.

**Access by settlement** (the gate that makes [[Settlements and Locations Template]] work):

| Rarity | Where it's sold | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common | "often be bought in a town or city" | SRD p. 205 |
| Uncommon, Rare | "usually found only in cities" | SRD p. 205 |
| Very Rare, Legendary, Artifact | only "in wondrous locations, such as a city on another plane of existence" | SRD p. 205 |

**Other magic-item rules worth knowing as an economy:**

- **Potion Miscibility** — combining potions requires a roll on a 1d100 table; a roll of 01 means "both potions lose their effects, and the mixture creates a magical explosion in a 5-foot-radius Sphere… 4d10 Force damage" (SRD p. 204). Mixing stock is a real business risk.
- **Consumables lose their magic when used**; scrolls crumble or fade (SRD pp. 204–205) — a consumable market is a treadmill, not a stockpile.
- **Magic items are at least as durable as mundane ones** and most have Resistance to all damage (SRD p. 206).
- **Artifacts can be destroyed only in some special way**, usually requiring "research or the completion of a quest" (SRD p. 206) — which is a history hook with a price tag (see [[History and Timelines]]).
- **Sentient items are NPCs with a price.** "When you make a sentient magic item, you create the item's persona much as you would create an NPC" (SRD p. 207), with random tables for abilities (4d6 drop-lowest), alignment, communication, senses, and purpose — including **Glory Seeker**, "seeks renown as the greatest magic item in the world" (SRD p. 208). A sentient item is an [[NPC Framework]] entry that happens to be loot.

This table is also your **campaign's economy dial**: it tells you what WotC thinks a tier-appropriate adventurer's liquid wealth is, which is the fastest way to sanity-check your own prices.

## Downtime: what the SRD does and doesn't do

**What it has:** the word appears only as *rest*. "A Long Rest is a period of extended downtime—at least 8 hours" (SRD p. 185) and "A Short Rest is a 1-hour period of downtime" (SRD p. 187). There is **no downtime activity list** — no carousing, no research project, no training montage, no business to run.

**What you can build from existing rules:**

| Downtime want | Use this SRD rule instead | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I practice my craft" | Crafting Nonmagical Items: 8 hrs/day, cost ÷ 10 days | SRD p. 103 |
| "I brew potions" | Brewing Potions of Healing: Herbalism Kit, 25 GP, 1 day | SRD p. 103 |
| "I copy spells onto scrolls" | Scribing Spell Scrolls table (above) | SRD p. 103 |
| "I hunt for components for a magic item" | Raw-material rolls: 75% city / 25% elsewhere, re-check after 7 days | SRD p. 207 |
| "I research an artifact / a curse" | No rule — adjudicate with History/Arcana Study checks (SRD pp. 9–10); artifact destruction "usually requires research or the completion of a quest" (SRD p. 206) | SRD pp. 9–10, 206 |
| "I live modestly for a month" | Lifestyle Expenses, paid at the start of each week or month | SRD p. 101 |
| "I take a commission" | Hireling rates define the floor: skilled 2 GP/day, untrained 2 SP/day | SRD p. 102 |
| "I rest and heal" | Long Rest / Short Rest (the SRD's only downtime) | SRD pp. 185, 187 |

## Confirmed [GAP]s in this area

1. **No downtime activities.** `downtime` = 2 hits, both rest definitions (SRD pp. 185, 187). `carouse` = 0 hits. *Official confirmation:* the [Wizards SRD FAQ](https://dnd.wizards.com/resources/systems-reference-document) records that 5.2.1 deliberately **removed the "Between Adventures" section** — this gap is by design, not an extraction error.
2. **No research, training, or practice rules** beyond crafting — `research` appears only as flavor (Wizard's "arcane research," SRD p. 78) and in the artifact-destruction sentence (SRD p. 206).
3. **No treasure-hoard tables.** Magic items are "gleaned from the hoards of felled monsters or discovered in long-lost vaults" (SRD p. 204) — but there is no hoard-by-CR table to roll on.
4. **No trade-goods price list.** The resale rule names "gems and art objects" (SRD p. 89) but prices none of them.
5. **No income, business, or property rules** (beyond lifestyle costs and the Ship Repair line).
6. **No market variance, haggling, or supply rules** — only the 75%/25% raw-material roll (SRD p. 207) and the "service rather than coin" clause (SRD p. 205).
7. **No infection, disease, or slow-healing economy.** Healing is handled by rests and spells, not by medical bills.
