# History and Timelines

The SRD has **no history chapter, no calendar, and no holiday rules** — verified by full-text search (see the [GAP] list below). What it has instead is a **skill, a definition, and a bag of hooks**: the History check (SRD p. 9), a glossary entry that scopes what History covers (SRD p. 189), and a d100 trinket table that is quietly a set of unresolved pasts (SRD pp. 26–27).

> [!tip] How to use this note
> The SRD gives you the *question-asking* machinery — checks, Study actions, background questions, curses. You supply the *answers*: five dated events, one live consequence, one ruin. Then you hang mechanics off them.

## What the SRD actually gives you

### 1. The History skill and its glossary scope

> [!src] SRD p. 9 — History
> Intelligence (History): "Recall lore about historical events, people, nations, and cultures."

> [!src] SRD p. 189 — History [glossary]
> "Historic events and people, ancient civilizations, wars, and certain creatures."

That glossary line is the closest thing to a design brief in the whole document: **events, people, nations, cultures, civilizations, wars, certain creatures.** Build your timeline around those six words and every entry will be checkable in play.

You can also *ask* for history rather than be told it: the **Study** action calls for an Intelligence (Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature, or Religion) check (SRD p. 10), and a *Book* rewards the same checks — "you gain a +5 bonus to Intelligence (Arcana, History, Nature, or Religion) checks you make about that topic" (SRD p. 96).

### 2. Character-level past: "Imagine Your Past and Present"

> [!src] SRD p. 20 — Imagine Your Past and Present
> "Let your character's background and species inspire how you imagine their past. That past fed into the character's present… Who raised you? Who was your dearest childhood friend? … Did you join an organization, such as a guild or religion? If so, are you still a member of it? What elements of your past inspire you to go on adventures now?"

This is the SRD's built-in hook into [[Factions and Organizations Template]] — and into your timeline. If three of the five party members answer "I joined a guild," your history needs a guild, and that guild needs a founding date.

Backgrounds themselves are "characteristics that represent the place and time" of a character's life (SRD p. 83), and custom backgrounds are a supported procedure (SRD p. 192).

### 3. Trinkets: unresolved history in an object (SRD pp. 26–27)

A d100 table the GM is explicitly allowed to use ("The GM might also use this table. It can help stock a room in a dungeon or fill a creature's pockets" — SRD p. 26). Ten entries that *are* plot hooks:

| d100 | Trinket | The history it implies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 03 | A gold coin minted in an unknown land | A polity nobody in the party can place |
| 04 | A diary written in a language you don't know | History locked behind a Languages check (SRD p. 20) |
| 10 | The deed for a parcel of land in a realm unknown to you | Ownership, inheritance, a border that moved |
| 42 | A rank insignia from a lost legionnaire | A dissolved army, still paying out grudges |
| 43 | A silver bell without a clapper | A temple or watch that no longer rings |
| 56 | An old key | A door — find it, or find out why it's moot |
| 57 | An indecipherable treasure map | Somebody already failed at this |
| 79 | An invitation to a party where a murder happened | A dated social event with a body |
| 82 | Half a floor plan for a temple, a castle, or another structure | A building that exists only in fragments |
| 85 | A diary with seven missing pages | Deliberate erasure |
| 87 | An iron holy symbol devoted to an unknown god | A faith with no living clergy (see [[Pantheon and Deities Template]]) |
| 88 | A book about a legendary hero's rise and fall, with the last chapter missing | The ending was removed on purpose |
| 90 | An ancient arrow of elven design | A battlefield, or an old war |

### 4. Consequences with rules attached

Three SRD systems make a past *bite*:

- **Narrative Curses** (SRD p. 193): "a broken vow, a defiled tomb, or a murdered innocent." Each is a *historical act with a mechanic still running* — this is the SRD's one purpose-built "history has teeth" rule.
- **Artifacts** (SRD p. 206): "Learning how to destroy an Artifact usually requires research or the completion of a quest." The artifact is a survivor of your first age.
- **The Mysterious Deck** (SRD pp. 231–232): cards named *Ruin*, *Throne*, *Key*, *Rogue*, *Sage*, *Flames* — each is a one-roll rewrite of a character's history. *Throne* even grants "proficiency and Expertise in your choice of History, Insight, Intimidation, or Persuasion" (SRD p. 232). (The item is renamed from *Deck of Many Things* in SRD 5.2 "to avoid using protected trademarks" — official SRD 5.2 FAQ, [Wizards' SRD page](https://dnd.wizards.com/resources/systems-reference-document).)

### 5. The SRD's clock is functional, not calendrical

Where the document *does* measure time, it measures it in working units:

| Unit | What uses it | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 hours | A Long Rest; a day of crafting or scribing work | SRD pp. 103, 185 |
| 1 hour | A Short Rest; a day's travel = typically 8 hours | SRD pp. 187, 192 |
| 24 hours | Influence-lockout after a failed check; item out of range ends attunement | SRD pp. 184, 103 |
| 7 days | Minimum wait before re-checking magic-item raw material availability | SRD p. 207 |
| **week or month** | "At the start of each week or month (GM's choice), choose a lifestyle" — and pay it | SRD p. 101 |

**That last row is your calendar seed.** The SRD leaves the week-or-month choice to the GM, never defines a month's length, and never names a holiday. Decide your unit once, write it down, and everything else (lifestyle costs, crafting days, market re-checks) hangs off it.

**Rules for writing it (mine, not the SRD's):**

1. Five entries minimum: origin, war, ruin, recent event, open wound.
2. Every entry must be *findable* — a History check (SRD p. 9), a Study action (SRD p. 10), a trinket (SRD pp. 26–27), or a named NPC who was there.
3. Every entry must be *reachable* — at least one has a mechanic attached: a Narrative Curse (SRD p. 193), a faction's claim ([[Factions and Organizations Template]]), or a site with traps (SRD pp. 199–201).

## Making a History check matter at the table

1. **Ask, don't tell.** "Make an Intelligence (History) check" (SRD p. 9), then give three tiers: success (the real event), partial (the version the winners wrote), failure (the rumor that everyone believes).
2. **Use the Study action for deliberate digging** (SRD p. 10) — it costs an action, so searching a library is a real expenditure.
3. **Reward the Book.** "If you consult an accurate nonfiction Book about its topic, you gain a +5 bonus to Intelligence (Arcana, History, Nature, or Religion) checks you make about that topic" — 25 GP, SRD p. 96. Put one in the ruin.
4. **Give the languages a job.** The Rare Languages list includes Thieves' Cant, Druidic, Sylvan, and others (SRD p. 20); an unreadable diary is a History check away from being a quest.
5. **Pay it off as a curse or a claim.** History that doesn't change a die roll or a faction's posture is flavor — keep it, but build at least one entry that does both.
