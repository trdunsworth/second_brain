# Worldbuilding Checklist

Work down this list before session 1. Each item links to the note that does the actual work and to the SRD rule (if any) that constrains the decision. Items marked **[GAP]** are unconstrained by SRD 5.2.1 — you are free, and required, to invent them.

> [!tip] Order matters
> Scope → cosmology → gods → politics → places → people → prices → sessions. Later decisions are cheaper to change when the earlier ones are settled.

## 1. Scope the campaign

- [ ] Pick a **tier of play** and write it in one sentence. Tier 1: threats to *local farmsteads or villages*. Tier 2: *cities and kingdoms*. Tier 3: *whole regions*. Tier 4: *the world or the multiverse* (SRD pp. 23–24).
- [ ] Decide how the group levels: XP and milestone are both GM calls — the SRD gives the XP track and bonus feats above 355,000 XP but no "adventuring day" formula **[GAP]** (SRD pp. 23–24).
- [ ] Confirm the **starting level** and use *Starting Equipment at Higher Levels* (SRD p. 24) if above level 1: levels 2–4 normal kit; 5–10 500 GP + 1d10 × 25 GP + 1 Common item; 11–16 5,000 GP + 1d10 × 250 GP + 2 Common, 3 Uncommon, 1 Rare; 17–20 20,000 GP + 1d10 × 250 GP + 2 Common, 4 Uncommon, 3 Rare, 1 Very Rare.
- [ ] Agree the **three pillars** you'll actually spend time on: social interaction, exploration, combat (SRD p. 5).
- [ ] Read [[Free Resources Directory]] and pick your secondary sources now, not mid-session.

→ Detail: [[Worldbuilding Checklist#SRD coverage map]] · [[Encounter and Adventure Design]]

## 2. Build the frame

- [ ] Decide which **planes** your campaign touches and which ones are rumor (SRD pp. 209, 214–216) → [[Cosmology and Planes]].
- [ ] Decide the **degree of planar contact**: can mortals open gates, or only the gods and item-holders? Key SRD liminal items: *Cubic Gate* (one face is the Material Plane) SRD p. 216, *Candle of Invocation* (opens a gate) SRD pp. 214–215, *Amulet of the Planes* (random destination) SRD p. 209.
- [ ] Create the **pantheon** — no SRD deity list exists **[GAP]**; only the Life Domain subclass is in the rules (SRD p. 40) → [[Pantheon and Deities Template]].
- [ ] Assign each deity a **portfolio, alignment, suggested domains, and symbol** (the SRD's own format, used by the SRD 5.1 pantheon appendix) → [[Pantheon and Deities Template#Reference: SRD-listed deities and domains]].
- [ ] Decide how **holy symbols** are sold and priced — the SRD lists *Holy Symbol* at **Varies** (SRD pp. 95, 97), so the price is yours.
- [ ] Note that Channel Divinity is drawn "directly from the Outer Planes" for both Cleric and Paladin (SRD pp. 37, 54) — gods and planes are mechanically joined.

## 3. Politics and power

- [ ] Name the **realms** in your tier's radius and mark their neighbours **[GAP]** (no kingdom, treaty, or government rules in the SRD) → [[Nations and Politics Template]].
- [ ] Write down the **law** the party can break: the SRD's only explicit law text is that *"in some settings, laws prohibit the possession and use of poison"* (SRD p. 197) and the alignment descriptions' appeal to *law, tradition, or personal codes* (SRD pp. 21–22).
- [ ] Create **factions** with a goal, a resource, and an attitude toward the PCs **[GAP]** (no faction or renown rules) → [[Factions and Organizations Template]].
- [ ] Attach at least one faction to a **background** the players can take: Acolyte, Criminal, Sage, Soldier (SRD p. 83), or a custom background (SRD p. 192).

## 4. Places

- [ ] Classify every settlement as **village, town, or city** — the SRD uses these three tiers as the gate for services (SRD pp. 102, 205, 207) → [[Settlements and Locations Template]].
- [ ] Stock each settlement with **lifestyle, lodging, and meal prices** from the SRD (SRD p. 101) rather than eyeballing them.
- [ ] Decide what **spellcasting services** are actually available there: cantrip/1st/2nd = village, town, or city; 3rd–5th = town or city; 6th–8th = city; 9th = city (SRD p. 102).
- [ ] Decide whether **magic items** are bought and sold locally: Common items often in town or city, Uncommon and Rare usually only in cities, rarer still only in wondrous locations (SRD p. 205).
- [ ] Check **raw-material availability** for crafters: 75% in a city, 25% in any other settlement, re-check after 7 days (SRD p. 207).
- [ ] Map the **travel terrain** the party will cross — pace, encounter distance, foraging DC, navigation DC, and search DC are all given per terrain (SRD p. 192).
- [ ] Place at least one **location with a past**: ruins, a cursed tomb, a demiplane (SRD p. 122), a *Teleportation Circle* sigil (SRD p. 168) → [[History and Timelines]].

## 5. People

- [ ] Build the **NPC roster** — name, role, attitude (Friendly / Indifferent / Hostile, SRD p. 177), what they want, what they'll do about it → [[NPC Framework]].
- [ ] Give every named NPC a **stat block or a CR 0 Commoner shell** (Commoner: CR 0, 10 XP, SRD p. 275) — no SRD "NPC class" rules exist **[GAP]**.
- [ ] Pick a **voice and a tell** for each of the top five NPCs; the SRD's guidance is that the GM uses an NPC's personality plus the characters' actions and attitudes to determine reactions (SRD pp. 10–11).
- [ ] Decide **morale** rules — none in the SRD **[GAP]**; *Running a Monster* (SRD p. 255) covers tactics, not surrender.

## 6. Prices and downtime

- [ ] Anchor the **coinage**: CP 1/100 GP, SP 1/10, EP 1/2, GP 1, PP 10; fifty coins weigh a pound (SRD p. 89) → [[Economy and Downtime]].
- [ ] Write the **cost of living** for your setting: Wretched free, Squalid 1 SP, Poor 2 SP, Modest 1 GP, Comfortable 2 GP, Wealthy 4 GP, Aristocratic 10 GP per day, chosen weekly or monthly (SRD p. 101).
- [ ] Price the **services** you'll actually use: skilled hireling 2 GP/day, untrained 2 SP/day, messenger 2 CP/mile (SRD p. 102); spellcasting services per level (SRD p. 102).
- [ ] Set **downtime activities** from the SRD's three: crafting nonmagical items (raw materials = half purchase cost, 8 hours/day, cost ÷ 10 = days) SRD p. 103; brewing *Potion of Healing* (Herbalism Kit + 25 GP, 1 day) SRD p. 103; scribing spell scrolls (per-level time/cost table) SRD p. 103.
- [ ] Price magic items by **rarity** if you allow buying and selling: Common 100 GP, Uncommon 400 GP, Rare 4,000 GP, Very Rare 40,000 GP, Legendary 200,000 GP, Artifact priceless; halve for consumables other than spell scrolls (SRD p. 206).
- [ ] Use the **resale rule** as written: *"Equipment fetches half its cost when sold. In contrast, trade goods and valuables—like gems and art objects—retain their full value in the marketplace. 'Magic Items' has prices for magic items"* (SRD p. 89).
- [ ] Note the gaps: no treasure-hoard tables and no adventuring-day XP budget **[GAP]** (verified absent).

## 7. Sessions

- [ ] Build each combat with the **XP Budget per Character** method (SRD p. 202) → [[Encounter and Adventure Design]].
- [ ] Give every dungeon or ruin **traps with severity bands** (nuisance or deadly for a stated level range) and scaling tables (SRD pp. 199–201).
- [ ] Decide the **environmental rules** you'll use: deep water, extreme cold, extreme heat, heavy precipitation, high altitude, slippery ice, strong wind (SRD pp. 195–196).
- [ ] Decide whether **fear and mental stress** are on: Sample Fear DCs and Sample Mental Stress Effects tables (SRD pp. 196–197) — discuss with players at session 0 (SRD p. 196).
- [ ] Prepare **social encounters** using the Influence action (Charisma: Deception, Intimidation, Performance, Persuasion; Wisdom: Animal Handling — SRD p. 10) rather than freeform dice.
- [ ] Prepare **exploration** questions: the Search action covers Insight, Medicine, Perception, Survival; the Study action covers Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature, Religion (SRD p. 10).
